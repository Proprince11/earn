# 100 Claude Prompts for Freelancers

**The Pack That Pays for Itself in One Project**

A curated collection of 100 battle-tested prompts for Claude (and any LLM) covering the entire freelance workflow — finding clients, writing proposals, scoping projects, handling difficult conversations, invoicing, and protecting yourself legally.

---

## How to use this pack

1. Copy any prompt below.
2. Paste it into Claude (or ChatGPT / Gemini).
3. Replace anything in `[brackets]` with your actual context.
4. Iterate the output by replying with what to change.

Each prompt is designed to give you ~80% of a usable result on the first try.

## SECTION 1: CLIENT ACQUISITION (Prompts 1–20)

### 1. Cold outreach email (Upwork-style)
> Write a 4-sentence cold pitch from me to a prospect for a [type of project]. The prospect is [describe the company / role]. My experience: [paste 2 lines of background]. The pitch should: open with one specific thing about their business, propose one concrete next step, and not include the words "passionate," "synergy," or "leverage."

### 2. LinkedIn DM that doesn't sound like spam
> Write a LinkedIn DM I can send to [job title] at [company]. I want to start a conversation, not pitch. Reference one of their recent posts about [topic]. Maximum 80 words. End with a question, not a CTA.

### 3. Re-engagement email for a cold past client
> I worked with [client/company] [X months ago] on a [type of project]. We haven't spoken since. Write a friendly 3-paragraph email that re-opens the conversation without being needy or salesy. Goal: get on a 15-min call.

### 4. Upwork proposal that beats the templated ones
> Write an Upwork proposal for this job posting: [paste posting]. My relevant experience: [paste]. Format: 3 short paragraphs. Paragraph 1: address the specific pain in their post. Paragraph 2: outline approach in 2-3 bullets. Paragraph 3: one specific question that proves I read the post. Avoid: opening with "Hi", "Dear Sir/Madam", or my own name.

### 5. Find prospects in a niche
> Brainstorm 30 specific company types that would pay $[amount] for [your service]. Avoid generic categories like "small businesses." Instead, give me specific niches like "boutique fitness studios under 5 locations" or "Etsy sellers in the wedding stationery category."

### 6. Decode a prospect's website for pain points
> Read the following text from a company website: [paste]. Identify 3 likely operational pain points based on what they don't say. Then suggest one specific service offer that targets each pain point.

### 7. Reddit comment that drives traffic without getting banned
> Someone in r/[subreddit] asked: "[question]". Write a genuinely helpful comment (200-300 words) that answers their question completely. At the end, add ONE soft mention that I built a free tool for this at [URL]. No pitch language.

### 8. Twitter/X thread that turns into leads
> Write a 7-tweet thread on the topic: [topic]. Each tweet must end on a hook that pulls readers to the next. Final tweet contains a soft CTA to my tool / service. Audience: [describe]. Tone: contrarian but helpful.

### 9. Find decision-makers without a sales tool
> I want to reach the marketing decision-maker at [company]. Walk me through 5 free ways to find their name and email, ranked by effectiveness, with the exact steps for each method.

### 10. Networking message after a podcast / interview
> The host of [podcast/show] interviewed [person]. Write a 70-word DM I can send to [person] referencing one specific point they made, complimenting it without flattery, and proposing a 15-minute conversation about [related topic].

### 11. Pitch a guest post to a niche blog
> I want to pitch a guest post to [blog name]. Their audience is [describe]. Suggest 5 topic angles they probably haven't covered, write a 4-sentence pitch email, and explain why each angle would perform.

### 12. Outreach to small businesses on Google Maps
> Write a 4-sentence email to a [type of business, e.g., dental clinic] in [city]. Pain point: their Google Business profile has been inactive for 3 months. Service offer: I'll manage it for $99/month. Tone: helpful neighbor, not salesperson.

### 13. Referral request that doesn't feel awkward
> I just finished a project with [client]. They were happy. Write a short email asking for 2 referrals to similar businesses. Make it easy for them to say no without feeling guilty. Include 2 concrete examples of who I'm looking for.

### 14. Turn a past client into a case study
> I want to turn my project for [client] into a case study. Write me 8 interview questions to send them. Mix of: situation/results questions, emotional/process questions, and quotable-soundbite questions.

### 15. Cold email subject lines that actually open
> Generate 20 cold email subject lines for an outreach to [type of role]. Half should be question-based, half should be ultra-short statements. None should be clickbait. Rank them by likely open rate and explain the top 3.

### 16. Convert a Slack/Discord lurker into a lead
> I've been lurking in [community] for 6 months. I want to start contributing to convert eventually into clients. Suggest 5 types of high-value posts I should make over 30 days, with one example post for each.

### 17. Re-pitch after a rejection
> A prospect said no to my proposal because: "[reason]". Write a graceful 3-sentence response that keeps the door open without arguing. End by asking one question that might surface what they actually need.

### 18. Pitch yourself for a Twitter audience
> Write a Twitter / X bio that converts profile visitors into leads. My specialty: [niche]. My ideal client: [describe]. Maximum 160 characters. Include one credibility marker and one CTA.

### 19. Outreach using a recent industry event
> [Industry] just had [event/news]. Write a 4-sentence cold email that uses this event as a hook to start a conversation with [type of prospect]. Ground the email in something specific and avoid being opportunistic-sounding.

### 20. The "audit" lead magnet
> Design an audit-style lead magnet I can offer free in exchange for a 30-min call. Topic: [topic]. Audience: [audience]. The audit should look high-effort but be deliverable in 20 minutes.

## SECTION 2: PROPOSALS & SCOPING (Prompts 21–40)

### 21. Project proposal in under 1 page
> Convert this client conversation into a 1-page proposal: [paste notes]. Sections: problem, proposed approach (3 bullets), deliverables (with quantities), timeline, investment, next step. No filler language.

### 22. Three-tier pricing without sounding like a SaaS landing page
> Build a 3-tier pricing structure for [service]. Each tier should solve a different psychological objection: tier 1 = "is this worth trying," tier 2 = "I want the obvious best one," tier 3 = "I want the no-compromise version." Price the middle one to anchor.

### 23. SOW that protects me from scope creep
> Write a Statement of Work for a [type of project] at $[amount]. Include: deliverables, what's NOT included (be specific), revision rounds, change-request process, payment schedule, late-payment penalty, and IP ownership clause.

### 24. Quote a project I've never done before
> A prospect wants me to [describe project]. I've never done this exact thing. Walk me through how to estimate scope: ask me 8 clarifying questions first, then estimate based on my answers, then suggest a price using value-based pricing.

### 25. Justify a higher price
> A prospect said my quote of $[amount] is too high. They expected $[lower amount]. Write a 4-sentence reply that defends the price without discounting, anchors to the value/outcome, and offers ONE alternative (smaller scope, not lower price).

### 26. Discovery call agenda
> Build me a 30-minute discovery call agenda for a [service] prospect. Include: opener, 5 questions that surface budget/timeline/decision-maker, value bridge, and one specific call-to-action at the end.

### 27. Identify hidden requirements before they bite
> Read this client brief: [paste brief]. List 10 questions I should ask before quoting, focused on hidden requirements that typically blow up scope.

### 28. Fixed price vs hourly recommendation
> For this project, should I quote fixed price, hourly, or value-based? Project: [describe]. My experience level: [describe]. Walk me through the trade-offs and recommend with reasoning.

### 29. Retainer proposal
> Convert my one-off project with [client] into a $[amount]/month retainer. Outline: monthly deliverables, response-time SLA, what counts as in-scope, opt-out terms, and the language to use when proposing it.

### 30. Read between the lines of a client message
> The client said: "[paste their message]". What might they actually mean? List 3 possible interpretations and one clarifying question I should ask before responding.

### 31. Counter a client's "scope reduction" request
> The client wants to keep the same deliverables but reduce my fee by 20%. Write a 3-sentence response that protects margin: either reduce scope proportionally or hold price.

### 32. Phased project proposal
> Convert a $[X] project into 3 phases. Each phase should be independently valuable so the client can pause without wasting money. Outline the phases, deliverables, and pricing.

### 33. Onboarding checklist for new clients
> Create an onboarding checklist for new [type of client]. Include everything I need to receive in week 1: access, assets, brand info, decision-makers, sign-off process, communication preferences, and emergency contact.

### 34. Negotiate net-30 down to net-7
> A client's standard payment terms are net-60. I want net-7 with 50% upfront. Write a confident 3-sentence message proposing this. Reasoning: I'm a small business, not a creditor.

### 35. The "small ask" foot-in-the-door
> A prospect has shown interest but won't commit to a full project. Suggest 3 paid mini-engagements I can offer (under $500, 1 week, low risk to them) that lead naturally into the bigger engagement.

### 36. Client briefing document
> Generate a client briefing template for [type of project]. Sections: business goals, success metrics, target audience, brand voice, references they like, references they hate, constraints, decision-makers, deadline.

### 37. Handle the "we're going with someone cheaper"
> A prospect chose a competitor who quoted half my price. Write a graceful response that wishes them well, leaves the door open, and offers to step in if it doesn't work out — without sounding bitter or desperate.

### 38. Add a non-compete / NDA reminder
> Write the language I need to add to my contract that prevents the client from poaching my contractors / pulling my work in-house for 12 months without buy-out.

### 39. Client kickoff email
> Write the kickoff email I send the day a contract is signed. Tone: enthusiastic but professional. Include: thanks, recap of scope, links to shared docs, what I need from them by when, my next step, and timeline of the first week.

### 40. Mid-project status update
> Write a weekly Friday status update template. Sections: what shipped this week, what's planned next week, blockers/decisions needed, current budget burn, on-track yes/no with reason. Maximum 200 words.

## SECTION 3: PROJECT EXECUTION (Prompts 41–60)

### 41. Convert messy client feedback into actionable tasks
> Client sent this feedback: [paste]. Convert it into a numbered list of specific, actionable tasks. Flag any feedback that's vague, contradictory, or out of scope.

### 42. Reduce 5 hours of research to 30 minutes
> I need to research [topic] for a client deliverable. Outline: what subtopics matter, what sources are most credible, what biases to watch for, and the 5 specific questions I should be able to answer when I'm done.

### 43. Outline before I write
> I'm writing [type of document] for [audience]. Topic: [topic]. Length: [length]. Generate a detailed outline with section headings and 3 bullets under each, before I draft any prose.

### 44. Drafting partner: write the boring middle
> Here's the introduction and conclusion of a piece I'm writing: [paste]. Write the body that bridges them. Voice: [describe]. Length: [length]. Don't repeat the intro or conclusion.

### 45. Structured brainstorm
> I need to brainstorm [type of ideas] for [project]. Generate 25 distinct angles, organized into 5 thematic categories. After listing, mark the top 5 most original ideas.

### 46. Quality-check my own work
> Read my draft: [paste draft]. Identify the 3 weakest sentences, the 3 weakest arguments, and 3 things a client would push back on. Don't compliment me. Don't suggest fixes — just identify problems.

### 47. Translate jargon for the client
> My deliverable contains technical jargon: [paste]. Rewrite for a non-technical client without dumbing it down. Use one analogy where helpful.

### 48. Repurpose one piece into five
> I have a [type of content]: [paste]. Repurpose into: a Twitter thread, a LinkedIn post, an Instagram caption, a 2-min YouTube script, and a 5-bullet newsletter teaser. Each should stand alone and not feel templated.

### 49. SOP for a recurring task
> I do [task] manually every week. Write a Standard Operating Procedure I can follow in 20 minutes. Include: prerequisites, step-by-step actions, common mistakes, and quality-check criteria.

### 50. Prompt that helps me think, not just output
> I'm stuck on [problem]. Don't solve it for me. Ask me 7 questions that would help me think it through, then summarize what my answers reveal once I've responded.

### 51. Reverse-engineer a competitor's deliverable
> Here's a competitor's [type of deliverable]: [paste / describe]. Reverse-engineer the structure, hidden assumptions, and value props. Identify 2 weaknesses I could improve on in my version.

### 52. Project post-mortem
> A project just ended. Walk me through a 5-question post-mortem: what went right, what went wrong, what I learned about the client, what I'd price differently next time, what process I'll change.

### 53. Documentation that future-me will thank me for
> Help me write internal docs for [process / system]. Audience: my future self in 6 months. Format: numbered steps, screenshots noted as [SCREENSHOT: ...], common error states, troubleshooting checklist.

### 54. Diagnose a stuck draft
> I've been rewriting this paragraph 5 times: [paste paragraph]. Diagnose what's actually wrong. Is it the angle, the structure, the voice, or the underlying argument? Don't fix it — just diagnose.

### 55. Find the missing argument
> My piece argues: [paste thesis and main points]. What's the strongest counter-argument I've ignored? How would a skeptical reader push back, and how should I address it without weakening my position?

### 56. Test my deliverable against the brief
> Original brief: [paste]. My draft deliverable: [paste]. Score the deliverable against the brief on a 0-10 scale per criterion. Identify gaps.

### 57. The "explain it to a 5th grader" rewrite
> Rewrite this section: [paste]. Audience: a smart 12-year-old. No jargon. Use one concrete example.

### 58. Clean up structure mid-draft
> Here's a messy first draft: [paste]. Don't rewrite it. Just propose a cleaner section/heading structure that organizes what's already there.

### 59. Surface my own assumptions
> Read my work: [paste]. List 5 assumptions I'm making that I haven't justified. Be specific.

### 60. Pre-flight checklist before sending to client
> Generate a pre-flight checklist I run through every deliverable before sending. Categories: accuracy, completeness, brand alignment, formatting, file naming, legal/compliance, attachments.

## SECTION 4: DIFFICULT CONVERSATIONS (Prompts 61–75)

### 61. Decline a project without burning the bridge
> A prospect wants me to do [project]. I don't want to take it because [reason]. Write a 3-sentence decline that's warm, doesn't lie, and refers them to someone who could help.

### 62. Confront a missed deadline (you missed)
> I missed a client deadline. Reason: [reason]. Write a same-day email that takes responsibility, doesn't over-apologize, gives a concrete new delivery date, and offers one form of remediation if appropriate.

### 63. Confront a missed deadline (client missed)
> The client hasn't given me feedback I needed 3 days ago. The project is now blocked. Write a polite-but-direct email that clarifies the impact on timeline and asks for a new ETA from them.

### 64. Push back on creative direction
> The client's feedback is taking the project in a direction I think will hurt their results. Write a 3-paragraph email that disagrees professionally, anchors the disagreement in their goals, and offers an alternative.

### 65. Ask for the late payment (1st reminder, friendly)
> Invoice INV-[N] is 5 days overdue. Total: $[amount]. Write a friendly first reminder email. Tone: assume an oversight. End with payment options.

### 66. Ask for the late payment (2nd reminder, firmer)
> Invoice INV-[N] is now 14 days overdue. The first reminder got no response. Write a firmer follow-up. Mention late fee and timeline before I escalate. Don't threaten.

### 67. Ask for the late payment (final, formal)
> Invoice INV-[N] is now 30 days overdue. I'm preparing to involve a collection agency / lawyer. Write the final formal demand letter, with a 7-day deadline.

### 68. Renegotiate a fixed price after scope creep
> The original scope was [paste]. The actual work has grown to [paste]. Write the email that explains the change in scope, presents the revised cost, and asks for sign-off before continuing.

### 69. Fire a bad client
> I want to end my engagement with a client because [reason]. Write a professional termination email that completes my obligations cleanly, returns/transfers any assets, and ends the relationship without creating a legal exposure.

### 70. Apologize for a real mistake without crawling
> I made a real error in a deliverable: [describe]. Write an email that acknowledges it specifically, explains how it happened (factually, not emotionally), describes the fix, and prevents recurrence. Keep it under 150 words.

### 71. Decline scope creep without saying "no"
> The client just asked me to add [extra request] to an existing project. It's outside scope. Write a response that says yes-and: yes I can, and here's what it adds in time and cost.

### 72. Address a tone problem in client messages
> The client has been increasingly curt and short-tempered in messages. Project quality is fine. Write the email that names the dynamic without accusation and proposes a 20-min call to reset.

### 73. Refund request response
> A client is asking for a refund. They claim [reason]. My position: [position]. Write a response that's empathetic but firm, references the contract, and proposes a resolution short of a full refund.

### 74. Ghosted client follow-up
> A client went silent mid-project after good initial momentum. Last contact: [X days ago]. Write a re-engagement message that's caring but doesn't grovel. Goal: surface what changed.

### 75. Disagree with a powerful client
> The client's CEO is wrong about [topic]. The project lead trusts me. Write the email I send to the project lead that disagrees with the CEO without making the project lead look bad in front of the CEO.

## SECTION 5: BUSINESS OPERATIONS (Prompts 76–90)

### 76. Set my hourly rate
> I want to earn $[annual income target] from freelancing. My realistic billable hours per year: [number]. Calculate the hourly rate that gets me there, accounting for taxes, expenses, vacation, and unbilled admin time. Show your work.

### 77. Annual goal-setting (without the cringe)
> Help me plan freelance income for next year. Current monthly average: $[X]. Goal: $[Y]. Walk me through a backward plan: how many clients / projects / hours / pricing changes are needed each quarter.

### 78. Decide if I should hire help
> I'm considering hiring [type of help] at $[rate/hr]. My current revenue: $[amount]/mo. My current hours: [hours]/wk. Walk me through the math on whether this is worth it, including risk.

### 79. Build a personal financial buffer
> I want a 6-month emergency fund. Monthly expenses: $[amount]. Currently saved: $[amount]. Income: $[amount]/month variable. Walk me through how fast I can realistically build the buffer and what to do with it once I have it.

### 80. Tax-deductible expense audit
> I'm a self-employed [type] in [country]. List 25 categories of expense that may be deductible, ordered by likelihood I've missed them. Don't give legal advice — just remind me what to consider.

### 81. Should I incorporate?
> I'm a freelance [type] earning $[amount]/yr in [country]. Walk me through the trade-offs of incorporating vs staying as a sole proprietor. Don't recommend — just lay out the variables.

### 82. Pricing-page rewrite for a personal site
> Rewrite my pricing page for [services]. Audience: [target]. Goal: pre-qualify so I get fewer but better leads. Replace generic "Contact us for pricing" with specific anchor pricing.

### 83. Service offer that scales without me
> Help me design a productized service from [my expertise]. Constraints: must be deliverable in under 4 hours of my time per unit, repeatable, and priced at $499–$999.

### 84. Pick which platform to focus on
> I have limited time. Channels considering: [list]. My niche: [niche]. Audience: [describe]. Rank the channels by ROI for my situation and recommend ONE to focus on for the next 90 days.

### 85. Build a 30-day content calendar
> Build a 30-day content calendar for [platform] for [audience]. 1 post per day. Mix of: educational, contrarian, behind-the-scenes, social proof. Each post needs a hook line and a content body in 1 sentence.

### 86. Audit my "About" page
> Here's my About page: [paste]. Audit it from the perspective of a buyer deciding whether to hire me. Identify the top 3 trust-killers and the top 3 trust-builders missing.

### 87. Email newsletter starter strategy
> I want to start a freelance newsletter to convert subscribers into clients. Topic: [topic]. Frequency: weekly. Outline: positioning statement, lead magnet, first 4 issues, opt-in form copy.

### 88. Generate testimonials when none exist
> I haven't asked for testimonials. I have [N] past clients. Write the exact email I send to each to request one, with a script that makes it easy for them (specific questions to answer instead of "say something nice").

### 89. Process for handling new project intake
> Build me an intake process: from a lead form to signed contract. Steps, time-boxes per step, automations possible (Zapier-style), and what I should NOT automate.

### 90. Decide what to outsource
> I do these tasks every week: [list with hours]. My hourly billable rate: $[X]. Identify which tasks I should outsource, in what order, and at what hourly rate to break even.

## SECTION 6: GROWTH & POSITIONING (Prompts 91–100)

### 91. Find my unfair advantage
> Background on me: [3 paragraphs about your skills, work, weird facts]. Identify 5 unfair advantages I have that I'm probably not using in my marketing. Be specific. No platitudes.

### 92. Niche down without losing income
> I currently freelance for [broad audience]. I want to niche down to [narrow audience]. Outline: how to test the niche before fully committing, what existing clients to keep, how to reposition my site, and how to handle referrals from outside the niche.

### 93. Position against bigger / cheaper competitors
> Bigger / cheaper alternatives to me are: [list]. My differentiation: [describe]. Help me write 3 positioning statements that don't fight on price and don't sound like every consultancy.

### 94. Personal brand without becoming a "thought leader"
> I want to grow a personal audience without doing motivational threads or "5 lessons I learned" posts. Suggest 5 content formats that build trust through demonstrated work, not opinions.

### 95. The 5-minute weekly review
> Build me a 5-minute Friday review template. Categories: leads in/out, money in/out, hours billable/non-billable, one win, one loss, one experiment for next week. Designed to be fast, not aspirational.

### 96. Productize a service
> Pick this one service from my offerings: [service]. Productize it: fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline, asynchronous delivery. Sell it as a buy-button, not a custom quote.

### 97. Find the next-tier client
> My current clients pay $[X]. I want to attract clients at $[2X]. Identify what changes about: my pricing page, my portfolio, my outreach, my positioning, and my process.

### 98. Strategic "no"s
> List 10 types of work / clients I should systematically say no to in order to build a coherent reputation. For each, give the polite refusal phrasing.

### 99. The one-year freelance review
> A year ago I was: [describe state]. Now I am: [describe state]. Help me write an honest year-in-review post for my blog/Twitter that's specific (not "be consistent"), shows real metrics, and ends with one actionable takeaway my audience can use this week.

### 100. The 90-day reset
> I've been freelancing for [X months/years] and feel stuck because [reason]. Walk me through a 90-day reset: what to stop, what to start, what to fix in week 1, what to ship in month 1, and how to measure if the reset worked.

## How to extend this pack with Claude Opus

Every prompt above is a starting point. To get even better outputs:

1. **Always paste real context.** Generic prompts give generic answers. Paste actual emails, briefs, drafts, numbers.
2. **Iterate by reply, not by retry.** If output isn't right, tell Claude what to change. Don't run the prompt 5 times.
3. **Ask for the failure modes.** "What's wrong with this approach?" gets you 80% of a editor for free.
4. **Use Claude as a thinking partner, not an answer machine.** Prompts 50, 54, 91, and 100 all force Claude to ask you questions instead of monologuing.

## Bonus: The meta-prompt

When stuck, paste your messy thoughts into Claude with this prompt:

> Here's a tangled situation: [paste messy thoughts]. Don't solve it. First, restate the situation back to me in your own words to make sure you understand. Then ask me 5 questions that would clarify what I actually want. Then, only after my answers, propose 3 different approaches with trade-offs.

This single prompt is worth more than the other 99 combined.
